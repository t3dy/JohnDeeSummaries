Summary

The later diary material repeatedly intersects with Catholic officials, Jesuits, papal nuncios, and questions of orthodoxy. Dee and Kelley must explain or conceal their actions in contexts where private revelation can be suspect. The religious politics are not decorative background; they shape what Dee can say and who may judge him.
