The chapter also helps explain why Dee's career has invited such different interpretations. The summary also marks the limits of certainty. Dee scholarship often works with traces: surviving books, damaged reputations, printed claims, hostile reports, visual diagrams, annotations, and documents produced for patrons. These arguments are strongest when they explain how those traces were produced and why they mattered.

Finally, the chapter belongs to a larger scholarly correction. Dee was not only a thinker with doctrines; he was a practitioner moving through institutions, households, libraries, courts, churches, and European learned networks. That social motion is part of the intellectual history.
