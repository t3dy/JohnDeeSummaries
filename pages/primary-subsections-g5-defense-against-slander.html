Summary

The heart of the letter rejects the reputation of conjurer. Dee insists that his studies are godly, philosophical, and useful. He appeals to honest readers and authorities to distinguish learned inquiry from demonic practice. This is one of the clearest primary sources for Dee's public boundary-making.
