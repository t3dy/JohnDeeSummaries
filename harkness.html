AuthorDeborah E. Harkness
TitleJohn Dee's Conversations with Angels: Cabala, Alchemy, and the End of Nature
SectionIntroduction

Harkness frames Dee's angel conversations as a continuation of his natural philosophy rather than a late-career aberration. The Book of Nature is corrupt and decaying, and the angels provide exegetical and restorative tools for reading and healing it.
AuthorDeborah E. Harkness
TitleJohn Dee's Conversations with Angels: Cabala, Alchemy, and the End of Nature
SectionThe Colloquium of Angels: Prague, 1586

Uses Prague to show Dee as public prophet, communicator, natural philosopher, and political-religious actor. Reconstructs Dee, Kelley, scryers, household, instruments, revealed books, angels, patrons, and audiences.
AuthorDeborah E. Harkness

SectionBuilding Jacob's Ladder: The Genesis of the Angel Conversations

Explains how Dee's earlier work prepared the angel conversations: Scripture and Nature as books, Propaedeumata as optical/celestial causation, Monas as symbolic decipherment, and mathematics as mediator.

TitleJohn Dee's Conversations with Angels: Cabala, Alchemy, and the End of Nature
SectionClimbing Jacob's Ladder: Angelology as Natural Philosophy

Shows why angels could function as natural-philosophical mediators. Dee sought pure wisdom through lawful prayer, angelic hierarchy, optical mediation, and ritual discipline.

SectionThen Commeth the Ende: Apocalypse, Natural Philosophy, and the Angel Conversations

Places the conversations in apocalyptic culture. Nature, learning, church, and political order seemed to be decaying, making Dee's angelic reform urgent.
