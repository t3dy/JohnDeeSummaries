Introduction

Introduction is part of Harkness. key terms: Book of Nature; natural philosophy; angel diaries; historiography; apocalypse. Harkness frames Dee's angel conversations as a continuation of his natural philosophy rather than a late-career aberration. The Book of Nature is corrupt and decaying, and the angels provide exegetical and restorative tools for reading and healing it. The historiographical point is that Challenges Heilbron/Shumaker-style dismissals, builds beyond Yates and Clulee, uses Whitby and Sherman to connect diaries, library, marginalia, and communication. Source: Deborah E Harkness John Dee s Conversations with Angels_ Cabala Alchemy and the End of Nature Cambridge University Press.pdf, pages 1-8.

The Colloquium of Angels: Prague, 1586

The Colloquium of Angels: Prague, 1586 is part of Harkness. key terms: Prague; Budovec; Kelley; scryers; showstone; holy table; Liber Logaeth; Rudolf II; Pucci. Uses Prague to show Dee as public prophet, communicator, natural philosopher, and political-religious actor. Reconstructs Dee, Kelley, scryers, household, instruments, revealed books, angels, patrons, and audiences. The historiographical point is that Replaces private-eccentric Dee with socially embedded Dee; treats scrying, material culture, household practice, and patronage as part of knowledge production. Source: Deborah E Harkness John Dee s Conversations with Angels_ Cabala Alchemy and the End of Nature Cambridge University Press.pdf, pages 9-59.

Building Jacob's Ladder: The Genesis of the Angel Conversations

Building Jacob's Ladder: The Genesis of the Angel Conversations is part of Harkness. key terms: Jacob's ladder; Scripture; Propaedeumata; Monas; mathematics; optics; cabala. Explains how Dee's earlier work prepared the angel conversations: Scripture and Nature as books, Propaedeumata as optical/celestial causation, Monas as symbolic decipherment, and mathematics as mediator. The historiographical point is that Argues continuity between Dee's accepted natural-philosophical works and angelic work; links humanist textual crisis with natural-philosophical uncertainty. Useful terms for browsing this chapter are Jacob's ladder; Scripture; Propaedeumata; Monas; mathematics; optics; cabala. Climbing Jacob's Ladder: Angelology as Natural Philosophy

Climbing Jacob's Ladder: Angelology as Natural Philosophy is part of Harkness. key terms: angelology; Pseudo-Dionysius; Agrippa; Trithemius; prayer; hierarchy; showstone. Shows why angels could function as natural-philosophical mediators. Dee sought pure wisdom through lawful prayer, angelic hierarchy, optical mediation, and ritual discipline. The historiographical point is that Expands natural philosophy to include angelology; complicates magic/conjuration/invocation categories; treats prayer and ritual purity as epistemic practices. Source: Deborah E Harkness John Dee s Conversations with Angels_ Cabala Alchemy and the End of Nature Cambridge University Press.pdf, pages 98-132.

Then Commeth the Ende: Apocalypse, Natural Philosophy, and the Angel Conversations

Then Commeth the Ende: Apocalypse, Natural Philosophy, and the Angel Conversations is part of Harkness. key terms: apocalypse; Reformation; prophecy; Enoch; Familism; Rome; universal reform. Places the conversations in apocalyptic culture. Nature, learning, church, and political order seemed to be decaying, making Dee's angelic reform urgent. The historiographical point is that Integrates apocalypticism into history of science; shows that natural signs and sacred history were mutually interpretive in Dee's world. The True Cabala: Reading the Book of Nature

The True Cabala: Reading the Book of Nature is part of Harkness. key terms: cabala; divine language; Adamic language; Book of Soyga; Liber Logaeth; true names; Enoch. Argues that the angels revealed a true cabala of nature grounded in divine/Adamic language, true names, tables, gates, airs, governors, and Enochic material. The historiographical point is that Moves cabala from scriptural exegesis alone to natural-philosophical exegesis; critiques over-simple use of ?Enochian?; links language, essence, and natural power. Useful terms for browsing this chapter are cabala; divine language; Adamic language; Book of Soyga; Liber Logaeth; true names; Enoch. Adam's Alchemy: The Medicine of God and the Restitution of Nature

Adam's Alchemy: The Medicine of God and the Restitution of Nature is part of Harkness. key terms: alchemy; medicine of God; Paracelsus; restitution; corruption; resurrection; Adam. Completes the program: after reading nature through cabala, Dee receives restored Adamic alchemy and the medicine of God to heal nature's corruption. The historiographical point is that Places alchemy and Paracelsian medicine within natural philosophy; argues Dee's alchemy is restorative and eschatological, not merely gold-making. Epilogue

Epilogue is part of Harkness. key terms: Casaubon; reception; Ashmole; Hooke; Boyle; manuscripts; later reputation. Traces collapse of Dee and Kelley's partnership, Dee's later disappointment, manuscript survival, Casaubon's skeptical framing, and later antiquarian/natural-philosophical interest. The historiographical point is that Explains how reception history made the angel conversations seem marginal; contrasts Casaubon's warning frame with continued interest from Ashmole, Hooke, Boyle, and others. Useful terms for browsing this chapter are Casaubon; reception; Ashmole; Hooke; Boyle; manuscripts; later reputation.
